Over the last few months, I've set out to really get my life more sorted out and organized so I can manage my time better. I have tried out numerous apps and "productivity setups," trying to find that perfect order to bring my chaotic mind and life together. But nothing really worked until recently.

This isn't going to be one of those complex setups you often see productivity influencers promoting in videos and "courses." I wasn't a fan of Obsidian at first because I found it really overwhelming after watching a YouTube video setting it up so extensively. In my opinion, people watch videos about second brains and personal knowledge management and think they've found the holy grail of productivity. They fall into the trap of constantly looking for the perfect app and setup, trying to optimize their workflow when, in reality, they're just wasting time instead of focusing on what's important: getting stuff done.

My setup is very simple. Here's what I use for my workflow.

- Note-taking app: Has to support [backlinks](https://help.obsidian.md/Plugins/Backlinks) and provide a simple way to organize notes by topic. It should also sync across all my devices.
- Calendar app: Creating and editing events should be quick and easy. It should also support natural language processing (NLP) for dates and sync with my phone's calendar.
- Tasks app: Adding tasks should be quick and easy. It should support natural language processing (NLP) for due dates and sync across all my devices.

So here are my apps of choice and how I use them.

## Note-Taking App: Obsidian

[Obsidian](https://obsidian.md) is my app of choice for taking notes. As I mentioned in the intro, I had used it previously in a very complex and overwhelming setup for a week, which just didn't feel right. Almost all videos of Obsidian on YouTube were like that. So I had rejected the app back then.

Fast forward a few months, and I saw Obsidian gaining popularity again on Twitter and Discord, so I decided to give it another try. Only this time, I read the docs instead of videos.

### Why I Like Obsidian

Obsidian has an elegant user interface with one of the best, if not **the** best, Markdown editors out there, which makes the note-taking experience smooth and natural. My favorite part is that it has community plugins! With thousands of plugins available, it gives you tools to do pretty much anything.

But where Obsidian really won me over is privacy. Notes are saved in a local folder and never go to a database. It also doesn't store notes in a weird Obsidian-specific format, it's just standard Markdown! It allows you to own and control your data.

### How I Take Notes

I organize my notes using folders and tags in my Obsidian vault to group notes by particular topics. For example, I've recently gotten into homelabbing. So I created a new folder in the Obsidian vault, and in that folder, I have notes on different applications I want to self-host and all of the research I've done on each application. I also put the appropriate tags on each note.


![A demo of tags](/images/demo_tag.jpg)


### What I Take Notes Of

I take notes on pretty much everything.

- Any idea that comes to mind
- Anything interesting I come across
- I break down the key points of videos or blog posts that make me think
- Cool thoughts and random musings
- Research on various topics
- Planning blog posts and events

Also, Obsidian is simply the best Markdown editor I have ever used, so I just use it for writing anything.

To make note navigation even faster and more efficient, I use a plugin called [Omnisearch](https://github.com/scambier/obsidian-omnisearch). It enhances the search functionality in Obsidian with OCR and a smart weighting algorithm. Plus, it supports Vim keybindings, which I love. It reminds me of the [Telescope](https://github.com/nvim-telescope/telescope.nvim) plugin in Neovim.

I also like Obsidian's Graph View, which lets me visualize how my notes and tags are connected. It's a pretty cool way to see how everything in my vault comes together.

### Plugins

These are three of my favorite plugins:

- Minimal Theme Settings, which is the plugin to configure the [Minimal Theme](https://minimal.guide) for a clean and minimalistic UI.
- [Omnisearch](https://github.com/scambier/obsidian-omnisearch), which is a better and quicker search.
- [Notebook Navigator](https://github.com/johansan/notebook-navigator), which has everything organized like the Notes app on the iPhone.


Hopefully this shows that you don't need ten different plugins to make Obsidian functional. It's already enough for whatever you want to do.

## Tasks App: Todoist

When it comes to managing tasks, I prefer to keep my notes and tasks separate. While some people might use Obsidian for to-do lists, I find it more comfortable to use a dedicated task app.

My go-to task app is [Todoist](https://todoist.com/). [Despite to-do apps being broken](https://frantic.im/todo-for-robots/), Todoist _works fine_. It has a nice UI which allows for adding tasks quickly, even when on the go, thanks to its convenient widget. It has excellent natural language processing.

![A demo of Todoist's task](/images/todoist_demo.jpg)

I particularly enjoy the satisfying sound it plays when I complete a task. Todoist's subtasks feature is incredibly useful for breaking down complex tasks into smaller, more manageable steps, which also happens to be perfect for shopping lists and checklists.

I also use Todoist as a reminders app. A few days ago, I needed to buy movie tickets for my girlfriend and I, so I added a reminder in Todoist a week in advance, ensuring that I wouldn't forget to buy the tickets on the day.

## Calendar App: Google Calendar

[Google Calendar](https://calendar.google.com/calendar) is my preferred calendar app. It syncs with Apple Calendar and features a clean and simple UI for quickly creating and organizing events. It offers all the features you'd expect from a standard calendar app. It is visually appealing, and it does the job.
